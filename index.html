It’s getting warmer. And glaciers are losing mass.

Global temperatures have continued to rise over recent decades. As the climate warms, glaciers around the world are losing ice faster than they can regain it. Since 2000, this has resulted in a sustained decline in global glacier mass — a long-term response to a warming climate.

When glaciers retreat,
the landscape changes.

But the loss of glaciers is more than a change in scenery. As ice disappears, the systems around it begin to shift too. Rivers and freshwater supplies are altered, natural hazards can intensify, and newly exposed land reshapes entire ecosystems. For plants, animals, and people, glacier retreat can transform the places they depend on to live.

And what can happen when a glacier collapse triggers a deadly disaster.

NEPAL'S SHRINKING GLACIERS

Nepal is home to thousands of glaciers, spread across the high Himalayas. They store water as ice and feed rivers that continue far beyond the mountains.

But that frozen landscape is changing. Rising temperatures are causing glaciers across Nepal to retreat, thin and lose mass, reshaping high-altitude valleys and the lakes below them.

Between 1980 and 2010, Nepal lost roughly a quarter of its glacier area.

THE MOUNTAIN

North of Kathmandu, close to Nepal’s border with Tibet, the Langtang Valley cuts through some of the highest terrain in the Himalayas.

Above the valley rises Langtang Lirung, a 7,000-metre peak surrounded by glaciers, steep rock faces and deeply carved river valleys.

Glaciers in the Langtang catchment have been losing ice for decades. Nearby Lirung Glacier has recorded about 38 metres water equivalent of cumulative mass loss since 1990.

THE COLLAPSE

On the morning of August 26, a massive section of the high mountain slope suddenly gave way.

Ice and rock accelerated down the mountainside, breaking apart as they fell. Within moments, the collapse had become a fast-moving avalanche of ice, rock and debris.

What began high above the valley was about to become something much larger downstream.

THE FLOOD

The falling mass released enormous energy into the valley below. Ice, rock, water and sediment mixed together and surged into the river system.

As the flow travelled downstream, it picked up more debris and spread through the narrow valleys.

What began as a collapse high in the Himalayas had become a destructive flood moving toward settlements below.

THE DISASTER

The flood reached communities downstream with devastating force.

Buildings were swept away or buried beneath debris. Roads and bridges were destroyed, cutting off settlements across the valley. Hydropower facilities and other infrastructure were also damaged.

For the people living along the river, a geological event that began high on a remote mountainside had become a human disaster.
